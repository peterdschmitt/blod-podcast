Some states didn't wait for January. **KFF Health News**'s Rachana Pradhan reported on November 9 that [Nebraska rushed Medicaid work rules. Then a cancer patient was told he'd lost coverage](https://www.mynspr.org/npr-news/2026-11-09/nebraska-rushed-medicaid-work-rules-then-a-cancer-patient-was-told-hed-lost-coverage). "As of August, roughly 1,100 Nebraskans had been denied benefits because of Medicaid work requirements," she wrote. One of them, Eli McCreary, told her, "I can't walk. I can hardly breathe. I can't move around. What job am I going to get?"

A court has since stepped in, but only partway. **AP**'s Fatima Hussein reported on October 29 that a [Judge blocks Trump administration's Medicaid work requirement rule forcing proof of medical frailty](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/judge-blocks-trump-administrations-medicaid-work-requirement-rule-forcing-proof-of-medical-frailty). As she put it, "Stearns' decision does not strike down the work requirements as unlawful."

So the rule is coming, and the details of how you prove things matter a lot. Here's what to know and what to do before January.

## Who has to meet the rule

The rule generally applies to adults in the Medicaid expansion group. Starting January 1, 2027, you'll need at least 80 hours a month of work, community service, a work program or half-time school, and you can combine them. Earning at least $580 a month, which is the federal minimum wage times 80 hours, also counts.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> KFF counts 43 states that have to put the rule in place.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

## Who doesn't

CMS lists these groups as excluded:<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

- Parents, caretakers and family caregivers of a child under 14 or of a person with a disability
- Pregnant people and people getting postpartum coverage
- People who are medically frail or have special medical needs
- Veterans with a total disability rating
- People in certain substance use disorder treatment programs
- Former foster youth, and certain American Indians and Alaska Natives
- People already meeting TANF or SNAP work rules
- People under 19 and people enrolled in Medicare

## What the court changed

The administration had added a test requiring people who are medically frail to also show they're significantly impaired and can't work. The judge blocked that extra step. KFF explains that the ruling applies nationwide, and that people now need to show a condition in one of the frailty categories rather than also prove they can't work.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> KFF adds that in 2027, states may accept your own statement that you are medically frail, called self-attestation.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## How to protect your coverage

- **Update your contact information now.** Make sure your state Medicaid agency has your current mailing address, phone and email.
- **Open every letter.** If the state can't confirm you've met the rule, it must send a notice and give you 30 days to respond. Your coverage continues during those 30 days.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>
- **Ask for an exemption if one fits.** If you have a serious health condition, are caring for a young child, or fit another excluded group, tell your state, and ask what proof it accepts.
- **Keep proof.** Save pay stubs, volunteer logs, school enrollment letters and doctors' notes in one place.
- **Mark your renewal.** For many expansion adults, renewals move from once a year to every six months starting in 2027.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>
- **If you lose Medicaid, check the marketplace right away.** Losing coverage can open a special enrollment period. See [Lost your subsidy? 2027 options, side by side](/blog/lost-your-subsidy-2027-options/) for what plans may cost.

For everyday care during a gap, a direct primary care membership charges a flat monthly fee for visits with that practice's own clinicians. It isn't insurance and won't cover hospital or specialist bills, so it works best as a bridge, not a replacement.
