The window is officially open, and it isn't quite the sign-up season you remember. **NPR** carried a KFF Health News report on November 1, [It's ACA open enrollment time. Here are the changes for health insurance shoppers](https://www.nwpb.org/npr-top-stories/2026-11-01/its-aca-open-enrollment-time-here-are-the-changes-for-health-insurance-shoppers). Julie Appleby writes that everyone using an agent on the federal marketplace will need to give that agent a one-time confirmation code or link. Georgetown's Stacey Pogue had simple advice for anyone who relies on a broker: "I would reach out early."

The **AP**'s Fatima Hussein laid out the season the same day in [What to know about the Affordable Care Act's open enrollment period now underway](https://abcnews.com/Health/wireStory/affordable-care-acts-open-enrollment-period-now-underway-137746299). Her list of 2027 changes includes stricter enrollment checks and potential delays in working with a broker.

None of that has to trip you up. It just rewards people who start now. Here's a checklist for your first week.

## 1. Log in and look, even if you plan to renew

Open your HealthCare.gov account (or your state's marketplace) and see what your current plan will cost for 2027 and whether it's still offered. The NPR report says agents and other experts advise against letting your plan renew automatically. Prices, networks and drug lists change every year.

## 2. If you use an agent, get ready for the code

New this year, an agent or broker on the federal marketplace can't act for you until you approve them electronically. CMS made that a requirement as part of its anti-fraud changes.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Here's how it works:<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

- You'll get a one-time passcode or link by text, email or automated phone call.
- Each code works once and expires within 15 minutes, so be ready to share it when you're on the phone or in the office with your agent.
- Once you approve an agent, it lasts for the plan year unless you authorize a different one.

## 3. Find out whether you'll need to verify your identity

Some shoppers are flagged as "high-risk" and must prove who they are before they can authorize an agent. CMS lists three groups:<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

- people filing a new application, including anyone moving to a new state
- people with an active plan whose premium after tax credits is $30 a month or less
- people switching to a different or new agent

If the online identity check fails, CMS says you can call the Experian help desk, or apply on HealthCare.gov and upload documents.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> That can take time, which is another reason not to wait until mid-December.

## 4. Book help now

CMS put a temporary pause on registering new agents and brokers for 2027 if they didn't have a 2026 agreement.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> If you work with someone, confirm they can still help on the federal marketplace and get on their calendar. Free local assisters, found through HealthCare.gov, can also help you compare plans. They don't sell anything.

## 5. Ignore the ads and robocalls

The FTC is warning shoppers to watch for scam ads, phone pitches and text messages during open enrollment, and it reminds people that prerecorded health insurance robocalls are illegal.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> It also sent warning letters to 25 companies that market health plans or sell sales leads, flagging claims like calling a plan comprehensive insurance when it isn't, misstating costs, and fake offers of cash rewards. The letters don't accuse any recipient of wrongdoing.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

The FTC's advice is to start at HealthCare.gov or call the Marketplace Call Center at 1-800-318-2596, and to report problems at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

## 6. Gather what you'll need

Have these handy before you sit down: last year's tax return or a solid estimate of 2027 household income, the names of your doctors, and a list of your prescriptions. If you have a direct primary care membership, keep its details too. It isn't insurance, so you'll still choose a plan for hospital and specialist care, and DPC covers visits only with that practice's own clinicians.
