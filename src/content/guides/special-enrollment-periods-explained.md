Medicaid is changing fast in some states. **NPR** reported in July, in [In Nebraska, Americans begin to lose Medicaid under Trump's work rules](https://www.kunc.org/npr-news/2026-07-31/in-nebraska-americans-begin-to-lose-medicaid-under-trumps-work-rules), that Nebraska became the first state to remove people for not meeting new work requirements, which call for 80 hours a month of work, volunteering, school or job training.

California may see much bigger shifts. **KFF Health News** reported on October 16, in [Next Governor To Face Thorny Choices After California's Rapid Medicaid Expansion](https://kffhealthnews.org/elections/medicaid-cuts-medi-cal-insurance-immigrants-hilton-becerra-california-governor/), that more than 2 million Californians under 65 could lose health coverage over the next four years, and that "at least 730,000 residents have already disenrolled since June 2025."

If you're one of the people whose coverage ends mid-year, the next question is when you're allowed to buy a marketplace plan. Outside open enrollment, the answer is a special enrollment period, and the rules are worth knowing before you need them.

## First, the open enrollment dates

On HealthCare.gov, you can enroll or switch for 2027 from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Pick a plan by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. After January 15, "you can enroll in or change plans only if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period."<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Some states that run their own marketplaces go longer. New Jersey's runs to January 31, 2027.<sup><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup>

## What counts as a qualifying event

The federal rule gives you 60 days from the event to pick a plan.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Common events include:<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

- **Losing other coverage**, like a job-based plan, a parent's plan when you turn 26, or COBRA when it runs out. Here the window opens 60 days before you lose coverage and ends 60 days after, so you can line up a plan with no gap.
- **Losing Medicaid or CHIP.** You get 90 days after that coverage ends.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>
- **Getting married, having or adopting a child**, or a foster placement.
- **Moving** to a new ZIP code or county, usually if you had coverage for at least one day in the 60 days before.
- **Becoming a U.S. citizen** or leaving incarceration.
- **A change in your subsidy eligibility.** The regulation lets you change plans if you become newly eligible or newly ineligible for premium tax credits.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

## What doesn't count

Losing coverage because you stopped paying premiums, or ending it on purpose, doesn't open a window.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> HealthCare.gov is blunt about COBRA: "Voluntarily dropping COBRA doesn't count."<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> If you're thinking of quitting COBRA because it's expensive, do it during open enrollment, or wait until it runs out.

HealthCare.gov also says that moving only for medical treatment or a vacation doesn't qualify, and neither does a divorce or separation that doesn't cost you coverage.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## The low-income option is gone

Until 2025, people with incomes up to 150% of the poverty level could enroll any month. That option was paused from August 25, 2025 through the end of 2026, and in May 2026 CMS made the ban permanent: exchanges "will continue to be prohibited from offering the 150% FPL SEP after PY 2026."<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup> If you were counting on it, plan to enroll during open enrollment.

## Expect to show proof

If you enroll because you lost coverage, HealthCare.gov may ask for documents, and "you have 30 days to send the documents."<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup> You can't use the new plan until it confirms you qualify and you pay your first premium. A broader requirement to verify most special enrollments was set aside by a federal court in June 2026, re-adopted by CMS for 2027, and then paused again by the court in July. CMS says that pause doesn't change the existing check for lost coverage.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup>

## Where DPC fits in a gap

A direct primary care membership isn't insurance and doesn't need an enrollment window, so some people keep one going while they sort out a marketplace plan. It can cover routine visits, but it won't pay for a hospital stay or an ER visit, so it isn't a substitute for getting back on a plan. A membership also covers only that practice's own clinicians, so unless your current doctor is part of it, joining means switching.

If you're turning 65, Medicare has its own enrollment periods. "Medicare's Open Enrollment isn't part of the Marketplace."<sup><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup>

## Questions to ask yourself

- **When exactly does my current coverage end?** Your window can start 60 days before.
- **Did I lose coverage, or did I choose to drop it?** Only the first opens a window.
- **Do I have the paperwork?** A termination letter or notice with the end date will speed things up.
- **Does my state run its own marketplace?** Its dates and extra options may differ.
