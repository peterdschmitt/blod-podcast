Medicare's open season is already underway, and for millions of people it comes with a deadline they didn't expect. The **AP**'s Fatima Hussein reported on October 15 that [Medicare open enrollment starts now. These changes will arrive in 2027](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/medicare-open-enrollment-starts-now-these-changes-will-arrive-in-2027). She writes that the temporary Part D premium subsidy will end early, though CMS projects only modest price increases for most people.

The bigger change for many households is that their plan is simply going away. **NPR** asked on October 24, [Is your Medicare Advantage plan ending next year? Here's what to know](https://www.wunc.org/2026-10-24/is-your-medicare-advantage-plan-ending-next-year-heres-what-to-know). Emily Pisacreta reports that closing plans should have sent letters the first week in October, and that last year 2.9 million seniors were in Medicare Advantage plans that closed.

If you got one of those letters, or you're helping a parent who did, here's what happens next and what you can do about it.

## How many plans are ending

KFF looked at CMS plan data and found that 4.3 million Medicare beneficiaries will have their plan terminated at the end of 2026. That's 3.1 million people in Medicare Advantage drug plans and 1.2 million in stand-alone Part D drug plans. For stand-alone drug plans, it's 6% of enrollees, double the share from last year.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

Another 1.4 million Medicare Advantage enrollees are in plans being folded into another plan from the same insurer. They're moved over automatically and aren't counted as terminated,<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> but it's worth checking that the new plan still covers your doctors and drugs.

## What happens if you do nothing

You don't lose Medicare. If your Medicare Advantage plan ends and you don't join another one, you're switched to traditional Medicare automatically.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

What you can lose is drug coverage. Traditional Medicare doesn't include it, so you'd need to pick a stand-alone Part D plan if you want Medicare drug coverage.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> If your stand-alone drug plan is the one ending, you'll need to choose a new one too.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## Your deadlines

- **October 15 to December 7, 2026:** regular Medicare open enrollment, for everyone.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Choosing by December 7 is the simplest way to start January with no gap.
- **Through February 28, 2027:** if your plan is ending, you have a special window from October 15 through the last day of February to pick a new plan or switch to traditional Medicare.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>
- **January 1 to March 31, 2027:** the Medicare Advantage open enrollment period, when people in a Medicare Advantage plan can make one change.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## The Medigap right most people miss

Outside special windows, a Medigap insurer can often look at your health history before deciding whether to sell you a policy. When your plan ends, you get a way around that. People whose Medicare Advantage coverage is terminated qualify for a special enrollment period for Medigap with guaranteed issue rights.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

That window is short. Washington's insurance regulator, for example, says you can enroll in a Medigap plan within 63 days after your Medicare Advantage plan ends, and that the company must take you without a health screen if you choose one of the covered plan types.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> If you're under 65 and on Medicare because of a disability, Medigap may not be available to you at all, depending on your state.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

This may be the easiest chance you'll get to try traditional Medicare with a Medigap policy. Whether that's a better fit than another Medicare Advantage plan depends on your doctors, your drugs and your budget.

## Questions to answer before you pick

- **Are your doctors and hospitals in the new plan's network?** Medicare Advantage plans usually limit you to a network. Traditional Medicare works with any provider that takes Medicare.
- **Are your prescriptions on the plan's drug list, and at what cost?**
- **What's the worst year going to cost?** Look past the premium to the deductible and the out-of-pocket limit.
- **Is a stand-alone drug plan needed?** If you go to traditional Medicare, yes, unless you have other drug coverage.
- **Do you have a direct primary care membership?** It's separate from Medicare, but the rules for doctors who see Medicare patients matter. We explain them in [DPC and Medicare, what happens to your membership when you turn 65](/blog/dpc-and-medicare-turning-65/).

Medicare's Plan Finder lets you compare health and drug plans side by side, including premiums, costs and benefits.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Every state also has a State Health Insurance Assistance Program, or SHIP, with free counselors who help people with Medicare compare options.
