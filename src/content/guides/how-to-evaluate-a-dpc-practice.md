## How to use this checklist

Call or visit two or three practices near you. Most offer a free meet-and-greet. Ask the same questions of each, write down the answers, and compare. A good practice will be glad you asked.

You can find practices through the DPC Frontier mapper, the DPC Alliance, or a search for "direct primary care" plus your town. We do not rank or recommend specific practices.

## 1. Price and contract

1. **What is the monthly fee for me, and for each family member?** Most practices (76%) price by age.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Get the full family number.
2. **Is there an enrollment or annual fee?** Some charge a one-time fee. Fine if it is disclosed.
3. **How long is the commitment, and how do I cancel?** Month-to-month with 30 days' notice is common. Be wary of a 12-month lock-in with a penalty.
4. **Can the fee go up, and with how much notice?**
5. **Can I see the membership agreement before I pay?** If the answer is no, that is your answer.

## 2. What is included

6. **What visits, procedures and tests are included in the fee?** Ask for the list in writing.
7. **What costs extra, and how much?** Labs, generic drugs, imaging and procedures should have published prices.
8. **Do you dispense medications in the office?** Rules vary by state. If yes, ask for the price of drugs you take now.
9. **Do you have cash prices for imaging, specialists or surgery centers?** Many practices negotiate these. It can save hundreds of dollars.

## 3. Access

10. **How many patients does each doctor have, and what is the cap?** Mature practices mostly carry 400 to 700.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Ask how full they are.
11. **How soon can I get a sick visit?** Same or next day is the norm.
12. **Can I text or call the doctor directly? Who answers nights and weekends?**
13. **Who covers when the doctor is on vacation?**
14. **Will you coordinate with specialists and hospitals,** sending records and making referrals?

## 4. Money rules: HSA and Medicare

15. **Do you meet the IRS definition of a direct primary care service arrangement?** To keep you HSA-eligible, the fee must be fixed and periodic, no more than $150 a month for one person or $300 for a family, and cover primary care only.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Ask them to confirm in writing.
16. **Do you bill insurance for anything?** A hybrid practice may still be fine, but it can change the HSA answer. See [DPC and your HSA](/blog/dpc-and-your-hsa-2026/).
17. **Have you opted out of Medicare?** About 81% of surveyed DPC doctors have.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> If yours has, Medicare will not pay for any service from that doctor, and you sign a private contract instead.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Important if you are 60 or older.

## 5. Quality and fit

18. **Who will I see, and what is their training?** Board certification in family or internal medicine is a good sign. Check your state medical board's license lookup.
19. **How long has the practice been open, and how many members has it lost?** Stability matters. A practice that closes leaves you looking again.
20. **What do you tell members about insurance?** The right answer is that DPC covers primary care only and you still need a plan for emergencies, hospital stays and specialists.
