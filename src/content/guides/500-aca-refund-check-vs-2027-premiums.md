If an envelope from the Treasury shows up in your mailbox this month, it may hold $500. **ABC News** reported on October 1 that the administration had begun sending checks to roughly 950,000 marketplace customers: [Trump administration begins mailing out $500 ACA refund checks. Here's what to know](https://abcnews.com/Health/trump-administration-begins-mailing-500-aca-refund-checks/story?id=136915170). The same week, **CNBC** laid out why [premiums are likely to jump again in 2027](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/health-insurance-premiums-2027-inflation-aca-obamacare.html), and on October 5 **CBS News** asked the obvious question: [Trump is sending ACA and Medicare checks. Are they enough to cover rising healthcare costs?](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-medicare-aca-checks-healthcare-costs/)

Put those stories side by side and you get the real question for anyone who buys their own coverage. A check is coming once. A higher premium is coming every month. Open enrollment starts November 1, and for most people the 2027 price will be a bigger number than the check.

Here is what we know about the money, and how it compares with next year's bill.

## Who gets a check

The White House announced the payments on September 10. They go to people in the 30 states that use the federal exchange, HealthCare.gov, who paid the full premium themselves with no premium tax credit.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> ABC News reported that most recipients earn more than 400% of the federal poverty line, the point where the regular subsidy stops, plus some people below that line who received no subsidy.

For 2027 coverage, 400% of the poverty line is $63,840 for one person and $132,000 for a family of four.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>

The administration says the money comes from a surplus in the user fees insurers pay to sell on HealthCare.gov.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> If you live in a state that runs its own exchange, such as California or New York, you are not on the list. Neither are the roughly 18 million marketplace enrollees CBS News counted as left out.

The checks arrive by mail with a letter explaining them. None of the announcements we read say whether the payment counts as taxable income. If you deduct your premiums as a self-employed person, ask a tax professional how to handle it.

## How far $500 goes against a 2027 increase

Insurers' proposed 2027 rates show a median increase of 15%, across 276 insurers in all 50 states and DC.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Your own increase could be higher or lower. Here is what 15% looks like on 2026 national average prices for a 40-year-old.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

<div style="overflow-x: auto; margin: 0 0 24px">

| | Cheapest bronze | Benchmark silver |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 average monthly premium | $456 | $625 |
| With a 15% increase (rounded) | $525 | $720 |
| Extra each month | about $69 | about $95 |
| Extra for the year | about $828 | about $1,140 |
| Months of the increase $500 covers | about 7 | about 5 |

</div>

Prices climb with age. Older adults can pay up to three times what younger adults pay for the same plan,<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup> so for someone in their 50s or 60s the same 15% is a bigger dollar increase, and the check covers fewer months.

These are averages, not quotes. Your ZIP code, age and plan choice change everything. To run your own numbers, use the [stack calculator](/calculator/).

## What the check doesn't change

**Your subsidy eligibility.** The check doesn't move the 400% line or change the rules for who gets a premium tax credit in 2027. If your 2027 income will land near that line, the tools in [Lost your subsidy? 2027 options, side by side](/blog/lost-your-subsidy-2027-options/) matter more than $500. For example, HSA contributions lower the income the marketplace counts, and for 2027 one person can put in up to $4,500.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

**The calendar.** Open enrollment on HealthCare.gov runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Pick a plan by December 15 if you want coverage to start January 1.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

**Your renewal.** If you do nothing on HealthCare.gov, you are automatically re-enrolled for 2027, in your current plan or a comparable one, at the new price.<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup> Comparing plans after November 1, before the automatic renewal kicks in, is how you see the new number before it hits your bank account.

**The trade-offs.** A cheaper plan usually means a higher deductible. Pairing a bronze plan with direct primary care and an HSA works well for some people and poorly for others. Our [worked numbers](/blog/dpc-plus-bronze-worked-numbers/) show both kinds of years.
