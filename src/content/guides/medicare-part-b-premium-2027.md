States are stepping in on drug costs, too. **KFF Health News**' Noam Levey reported on November 13 that, [Fed up with the healthcare industry, this red state capped insulin prices for patients](https://www.wfae.org/health/2026-11-13/fed-up-with-the-healthcare-industry-this-red-state-capped-insulin-prices-for-patients). "North Dakota is one of 29 states that have enacted such measures," he wrote, though "The cap won't suddenly make healthcare affordable."

So here is what the new Medicare numbers mean for your monthly budget, and what to do with the time left in open enrollment.

## What you'll pay in 2027

CMS released the 2027 figures on November 17. The standard Part B premium is $209.40 a month, and the yearly Part B deductible is $292. CMS says the increase is "mainly due to projected increases in healthcare spending."<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

On the hospital side, the Part A deductible rises to $1,776 for each inpatient stay, up from $1,736. About 99% of people with Medicare pay no Part A premium because they have at least 40 quarters of Medicare-covered work.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## How much of your raise you keep

Social Security benefits go up 3.6% in January, which works out to about $75 a month for the average retiree.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> If your Part B premium comes out of your check, $6.50 of that goes back to Medicare, leaving a typical retiree roughly $68.50 more a month. That's a gentler squeeze than this year. KFF found the 2026 Part B increase took about $18 out of an average raise of about $56.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

Your own math may differ. If your benefit is below average, the same $6.50 takes a bigger share of your raise.

## Who pays more

If your income is higher, you pay an extra charge on top of the standard premium. CMS says it affects roughly 9% of people with Part B.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Here's the 2027 monthly total:

- **$112,000 or less** single, **$224,000 or less** joint: $209.40
- **Up to $141,000** single, **$282,000** joint: $293.10
- **Up to $177,000** single, **$354,000** joint: $418.80
- **Up to $212,000** single, **$424,000** joint: $544.40
- **Under $500,000** single, **$750,000** joint: $670.00
- **$500,000 or more** single, **$750,000 or more** joint: $711.80

## What to do before Dec 7

- **Check your bank deposit in January.** It should show the raise minus the new premium. If it doesn't, call Social Security.
- **Read your plan's Annual Notice of Change.** Premiums, drug lists and networks can change for 2027.
- **Look up your doctors and prescriptions** in the plan you're considering on Medicare.gov's Plan Finder.
- **Get a free second opinion.** Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers unbiased counseling.
- **Remember the deadline.** Changes made by Dec 7 take effect January 1.

If you use a direct primary care membership for everyday visits, keep in mind it isn't insurance and doesn't replace Medicare. See [DPC and Medicare, what happens to your membership when you turn 65](/blog/dpc-and-medicare-turning-65/) for how the two fit together.
