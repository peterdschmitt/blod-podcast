For a lot of people buying their own coverage, the bigger 2027 surprise isn't the price. It's the company. **NBC News** reported on October 29 that [Many with ACA coverage face a new crisis in 2027: Insurers fleeing the market](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/many-aca-coverage-face-crisis-153622093.html). Berkeley Lovelace Jr. writes that Cigna will leave all 11 states where it sells ACA plans, affecting more than 350,000 enrollees. KFF's Cynthia Cox told NBC, "The main takeaway is that more insurance companies are leaving the marketplace than coming in."

The same lesson is showing up on the Medicare side. **KFF Health News** reported on October 30 that [Medicare Drug Plans Are Hiking Premiums While Trump Claims Victory on Medicine Prices](https://kffhealthnews.org/medicare/medicare-drug-plans-premium-hikes-affordability-trump-cms/). Phil Galewitz writes that stand-alone drug plan premiums are set to jump about 50% on average, and that enrollees can avoid much of it by switching to a cheaper plan.

Whether it's Medicare or the marketplace, letting a plan roll over without looking is getting expensive. If you got a letter saying your marketplace insurer is leaving, here's what happens next and what to check.

## Who's leaving

As of October 29, KFF counted 11 insurers that plan to leave at least one state's marketplace for 2027, and seven that plan to enter a new state. Cigna is leaving the individual market in all 11 states where it sells today. KFF notes that exits can mean fewer choices of plans and provider networks, and can disrupt coverage for some enrollees.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

You should already know if this affects you. HealthCare.gov says that by November 1 you should get two letters, one from your current insurer and one from the marketplace.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> If you haven't seen them, check your HealthCare.gov account or call your insurer.

## What happens if you do nothing

You won't simply lose coverage. HealthCare.gov says that if your company isn't offering plans next year, it will re-enroll you in a plan with a different insurance company.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

The catch is who picks that plan. Under the federal rule, your state's insurance regulator can direct where you're moved. If it doesn't, the marketplace places you in a "similar" plan from a different insurer, to the extent state law allows.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Similar isn't the same. The new plan can have a different provider network, a different drug list and a different premium after your tax credit. If you use a state-run marketplace instead of HealthCare.gov, its process may differ, so check its site.

## Your deadlines

- **November 1, 2026:** open enrollment begins and runs through January 15, 2027.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>
- **December 15, 2026:** choose a plan by this date for coverage that starts January 1. If you don't act by then, you may be re-enrolled automatically for January 1.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>
- **January 15, 2027:** open enrollment ends. Picking a plan by this date gets you coverage starting February 1.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

Being moved automatically doesn't lock you in. CMS says that if your plan was canceled and your insurer already placed you in a new plan, you can still switch to a different marketplace plan during open enrollment.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

## Don't skip the first payment

This is the step that trips people up when the company changes. Your plan doesn't start until you pay the first month's premium, and you pay it to the insurance company, not the marketplace.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> If you were moved to a new insurer, the first bill comes from that company. Any autopay you set up was with your old insurer, so plan to set up payment again with the new one.

## What to check in the plan you're matched to

- **Are your doctors in network?** Search the new insurer's directory by name, and call the office to confirm.
- **Are your prescriptions covered, and at what tier?** Look each one up on the plan's drug list.
- **What will you pay each month after your tax credit?** CMS says more than 75% of people who buy through HealthCare.gov will have access to premiums of $50 a month or less in 2027,<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup> but the plan you're matched to may not be one of them.
- **What's the deductible and out-of-pocket limit?** A lower premium can come with a much bigger bill when you need care.
- **Did your income or household change?** HealthCare.gov asks you to update your application with expected changes, since they affect your tax credit.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

If you have a direct primary care membership, it isn't affected by your insurer leaving, since it's a separate agreement with the practice. What can change is how well your new plan's network lines up with the specialists and hospitals your DPC doctor refers you to, so check those too.
