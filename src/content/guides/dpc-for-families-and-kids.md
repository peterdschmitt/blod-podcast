If you're looking at direct primary care for your family, two questions come up fast: what will it cost for the kids, and what care do they actually get? Here's how practices handle both.

## What families typically pay

AAFP puts typical DPC fees at $50 to $100 a month for adults, $20 to $49 for children, and $100 or more for families.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> In the DPC Alliance's 2026 survey, 76.4% of practices set prices by age, 15.1% charge everyone the same, and 8.5% change the per-person price based on how many family members join.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

In practice, you'll usually see one of three models. Here are real examples from practices' own price pages, read on October 16, 2026. Prices change, so check before you sign up.

- **A child rate when a parent joins.** Family Tree Primary Care in Oviedo, Florida, charges $35 a month for a child 18 or younger with an enrolled parent, and $75 to $115 for adults depending on age.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>
- **A flat family price.** The same practice offers $240 a month for parents and dependents under 18, and caps its $100-per-person registration fee at $200 per family.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>
- **A cap on kids.** Hoke DPC in Oxford, Ohio, charges $40 a month per child when a parent is a member at $75. You pay for the first two children, and each additional child is free, so all your kids cost $80 a month.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

## A worked example

Take a hypothetical family: two parents, both 38, and two kids, ages 6 and 9. At Family Tree's listed prices:

- Per person: $75 + $75 + $35 + $35 = $220 a month, or $2,640 a year
- Family price: $240 a month, or $2,880 a year

With two kids, paying per person is $20 a month cheaper. Add a third child and per-person pricing rises to $255, so the $240 family price wins. At Hoke's prices, the same family pays $75 + $75 + $80 = $230 a month however many kids they have.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> Registration fees come on top in the first year.

## Pediatric-only DPC

Some practices see only children. Pediatricians make up 6.4% of DPC physicians in the DPC Alliance survey.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> These practices tend to cost more per child, especially for babies, who need the most visits.

Moon River Pediatrics in Savannah, Georgia, lists self-pay prices of $175 a month for a baby's first year, $145 from 13 to 36 months, $120 from 3 to 6 years, and $85 from age 7. Families with several children pay no more than $325 a month, plus a one-time $150 enrollment fee per family. The practice says the fee covers all well checks and unlimited sick visits, in person or virtual, and members can call or text a members-only number.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>

## The vaccine question

Ask this one before you join. Some DPC practices give routine childhood vaccines. Moon River says it gives routine immunizations on the American Academy of Pediatrics schedule.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> Others don't. Firefly Direct Primary Care in Wauwatosa, Wisconsin, says it isn't able to give routine vaccinations to young children, though it will help arrange them.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

If your child's practice doesn't stock vaccines, you have two common routes:

- **Your insurance.** Marketplace plans must cover well-baby and well-child visits and immunizations for kids. In most cases there's no copay or coinsurance, even before you meet your deductible, when an in-network provider gives them.<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup>
- **Vaccines for Children.** The federal VFC program covers children 18 and younger who are Medicaid-eligible, uninsured, underinsured, or American Indian or Alaska Native. There's no cost for the vaccines VFC providers give eligible kids.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup>

A DPC membership also covers only that practice's own clinicians, so unless your child's current pediatrician is part of it, joining means switching doctors.<sup><a href="#src-13">13</a></sup>

## Your HSA and the family limit

Starting in 2026, an HSA can pay DPC fees, and having DPC no longer stops you from contributing, as long as the arrangement meets IRS rules. One of them is a fee cap: $150 a month for one person, or $300 for an arrangement that covers more than one person, and those caps hold for 2027.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup> Our example family's $220 to $240 fits. A pediatric practice's $325 family maximum is over the line, so check with a tax professional before paying a fee like that from an HSA.

## Don't skip the insurance for your kids

DPC covers everyday care. It doesn't pay for a broken arm at the ER, a hospital stay or a specialist. Your kids still need real coverage for those, and they may qualify for more help than you think.

CHIP covers children in families that earn too much to qualify for Medicaid, and you can apply any time of year.<sup><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup> As of January 2026, the median income limit for kids' Medicaid and CHIP was 255% of the poverty line.<sup><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup> Rules differ by state, so check yours even if you don't qualify yourself.

## Questions to ask a practice

- **What does a child cost with and without a parent enrolled, and is there a family cap?**
- **Do you give routine childhood vaccines, and do you take part in Vaccines for Children?**
- **Who sees my child: a pediatrician, a family doctor, or another clinician?**
- **What's included: well checks, sick visits, sports physicals, after-hours texting?**
- **Is there a registration fee, and is it per person or per family?**
