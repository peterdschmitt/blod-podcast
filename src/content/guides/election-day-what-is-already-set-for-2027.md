Health care is on the ballot in a lot of places this year, if not by name. **NPR**'s Maham Javaid reported on October 31 that [A lot of Floridians lost their healthcare this year. Now, they're heading to the polls](https://www.wskg.org/npr-news/2026-10-31/a-lot-of-floridians-lost-their-healthcare-this-year-now-theyre-heading-to-the-polls). Nearly 443,000 fewer people had coverage in Florida in 2026, according to federal enrollment data, and premiums are expected to rise again as open enrollment begins.

On the Medicaid side, **KFF Health News**'s Jackie Fortiér reported on November 2 that [A GOP Congressman Backed Medicaid Work Requirements. His Voters Might Be Exempt.](https://kffhealthnews.org/elections/medicaid-work-rules-california-valadao-villegas-22nd-district-unemployment-exempt) She explains that the new rules require many adults to work, volunteer or go to school for at least 80 hours a month, and that states can seek exemptions for counties with high unemployment.

We don't take sides on candidates. But whoever wins, new laws take time, and several changes for 2027 are already on the calendar. Here's what's set and what you can do now.

## What's already set for marketplace coverage

The extra pandemic-era tax credits are gone, and fewer people are buying marketplace plans. KFF found that enrollment fell from 21.8 million people in 2025 to 19.2 million in February 2026, a 12% drop, and expects it to keep declining.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> ABC News reports that KFF projects benchmark silver premiums will rise about 13% on average for 2027.

Any new law that changed tax credits would need to pass Congress and be carried out by the agencies. If you're shopping now, plan around the prices and rules you see on HealthCare.gov today.

## What's already set for Medicaid

Starting January 1, 2027 or earlier, 44 states including D.C. must put Medicaid work requirements in place.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> CMS says the rule generally covers adults ages 19 to 64 in the Medicaid expansion group. They'll need 80 hours a month of work, community service, a work program or at least half-time school, and the state checks at application and at renewal.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

Many people are exempt. CMS lists groups including parents and caregivers of a young child or a person with a disability, people who are medically frail, pregnant and postpartum people, veterans with a total disability rating, and people in addiction treatment.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> KFF notes that the Congressional Budget Office estimated the work requirements would leave over 5 million more people uninsured by 2034.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## What you can do now, whatever happens

- **Update your income on your marketplace application.** HealthCare.gov warns that if your income goes up and you don't report it, you'll have to pay back the difference in tax credits when you file your taxes.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>
- **If your income drops, check Medicaid.** You can apply for Medicaid and CHIP any time of year.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>
- **If you're on Medicaid, learn the work rule now.** Find out whether you're exempt, and keep pay stubs or school records handy for your renewal.
- **Shop before the deadlines.** Pick a marketplace plan by December 15 for January 1 coverage. Medicare enrollees can change plans through December 7.
- **Keep your everyday care steady.** A direct primary care membership isn't insurance and doesn't depend on Medicaid or marketplace rules, but it covers visits only with that practice's own clinicians. You'll still want coverage for hospital and specialist care.
