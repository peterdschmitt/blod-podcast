A lot of people are reshuffling their coverage this fall, and not always by choice. On September 22 the **Associated Press** reported that the [Trump administration to remove 760,000 Affordable Care Act enrollees over fraud claims](https://abcnews.com/US/wireStory/trump-administration-remove-760000-affordable-care-act-enrollees-136657561). The cancellations cover about 315,000 enrollments, and another 419,000 enrollments will get extra checks.

**NPR** covered the same day from the enrollee's side: [HHS cancels health insurance of 760,000 individuals enrolled in healthcare.gov plans](https://www.kaxe.org/2026-09-22/hhs-cancels-health-insurance-of-760-000-individuals-enrolled-in-healthcare-gov-plans). KFF's Cynthia Cox told NPR that people who didn't answer their insurer within 30 days had their plans canceled, and officials said 400,000 more enrollees might be next.

Whether you lost a plan, dropped one over price, or just dread your renewal notice, you'll probably hear about catastrophic plans while you shop. They're cheap for a reason, but unlike many low-cost options, they're real ACA insurance. The catch for 2027 is who's allowed to buy one.

## What a catastrophic plan is

A catastrophic plan has a very high deductible, and that deductible is also the most you can pay in a year for covered care. For 2027 that's $12,000 for one person.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Before you hit it, the plan still pays for preventive care and for some of the cost of up to three primary care visits a year.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

It keeps the ACA protections that short-term plans and sharing ministries don't. A pre-existing condition can't get you turned down, and there's a hard cap on what you owe. Since 2026, a catastrophic plan bought on the marketplace also lets you put money in a health savings account.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

Two limits matter. You can't use a premium tax credit on one.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> And they aren't sold everywhere: in 2026, insurers offered them in 36 states and DC, down from 40 states the year before.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup>

## What the court paused

In September 2025, CMS opened a new door. For 2026 and later years, people who couldn't get a tax credit because of their income (below 100% or above 250% of the poverty line) could claim a hardship exemption and buy a catastrophic plan at any age.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> More than 20,000 people who didn't qualify for tax credits used it last open enrollment.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

Then, on July 16, 2026, a federal judge in Maryland stayed eight provisions of the 2027 marketplace rule. One was the expansion of catastrophic eligibility, along with that September 2025 guidance.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> The judge found that defining a hardship by income alone was likely unlawful.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> CMS has said more guidance is coming. For now, if you're 30 or older, you need one of the older exemptions to buy a catastrophic plan for 2027.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Who qualifies for 2027

**Under 30:** you can buy one, no exemption needed.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup>

**30 or older:** you need one of two exemptions.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

- **Hardship.** HealthCare.gov lists situations such as homelessness, an eviction or foreclosure, a utility shut-off notice, domestic violence, a death in the family, a fire or flood, bankruptcy, unpaid medical bills that left you in substantial debt, and rising costs from caring for an ill, disabled or aging relative.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup>
- **Affordability.** You qualify if the cheapest coverage available to you would cost more than a set share of your household income.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup> For 2027, that share is 8.50%.<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup> HealthCare.gov's exemption page still shows an older percentage, so check the current figure when you apply.

## How to get the exemption

You apply for the exemption first. If it's approved, you get an exemption certificate number, which you use to enroll in the catastrophic plan.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup> The forms and instructions are on HealthCare.gov, and the help line is 1-800-318-2596.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup> If you live in California, Connecticut, Maryland or DC, apply through your state's exchange instead.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

Leave yourself time. Open enrollment runs November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> Pick a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1.

## Two hypothetical shoppers

**Maya, 27, freelance designer.** She doesn't need an exemption. In 2026, the average catastrophic plan for a 27-year-old cost $346 a month, while the cheapest bronze plan cost $369 before any tax credit.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup> That's a gap of $23 a month, or $276 a year. If Maya qualifies for a tax credit, the credit works on bronze but not on catastrophic, so bronze could end up cheaper. If she doesn't, the catastrophic plan saves her a little on premiums and gives her the same $12,000 worst case.

**Daniel, 58, early retiree.** He expects $70,000 of income in 2027, which is above the subsidy cutoff for one person. His affordability line is 8.50% of $70,000:

- $70,000 × 0.085 = $5,950 a year, or about $496 a month

Say the cheapest plan in his area costs $720 a month. That's above his line, so he could apply for an affordability exemption. Under the income-only route that's now paused, his income alone would have qualified him for a hardship exemption. For 2027 he has to show the numbers.

These are examples, not advice. Your prices depend on your ZIP code, age and income, so check HealthCare.gov.

## Where DPC fits

A catastrophic plan already covers up to three primary care visits a year, so a direct primary care membership overlaps with part of it.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Where DPC adds the most is everything else: visits after the third, messages with your doctor, and the routine care you'd otherwise pay full price for under a $12,000 deductible. One trade-off to know: the membership only covers that practice's own clinicians, so unless your current doctor is part of it, joining means switching.<sup><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup>

Because a marketplace catastrophic plan now works with an HSA, you can pay a qualifying DPC fee from it, up to $150 a month for one person or $300 for a family in 2027. HSA contribution limits for 2027 are $4,500 for one person and $9,000 for a family.<sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup> To see how the fee and the deductible add up, try the [stack calculator](/calculator/).
