## What changed

Until this year, the IRS treated a DPC membership as a second health plan, one that paid for care before your deductible. Having one generally meant you couldn't put money into an HSA, even if your insurance plan otherwise qualified.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act ended that conflict for what it calls a "direct primary care service arrangement." Starting January 1, 2026, you can have one and still contribute.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> There are two conditions. The arrangement has to cover primary care only, for a fixed periodic fee, and the fees have to stay under a monthly cap.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

The caps are tied to inflation after 2026, but the IRS left them at $150 and $300 for 2027.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The limit applies to all your DPC fees combined. If they go over, the fees are still a medical expense, but you lose the right to contribute to an HSA.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## What qualifies

The care has to come from a primary care clinician: a doctor in family, internal, geriatric or pediatric medicine, or a nurse practitioner, clinical nurse specialist or physician assistant. Here's where the IRS draws the line on services:<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

A practice can still bill you, or your insurer, for care it provides outside the membership. That doesn't disqualify the arrangement, as long as the fixed fee is the only payment for the care the membership covers.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Ask the practice which services sit inside the fee and which don't.

## Paying from your HSA

HSA money can now pay a qualifying DPC fee tax-free.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> At the same time, every individual-market bronze and catastrophic plan became HSA-compatible this year, even ones that don't meet the usual high-deductible rules.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> Put those together and you get the pairing people are talking about: a DPC membership alongside a bronze or catastrophic plan, with the fee paid from the HSA.

Whether that works for your household depends on your health, your income and what's available where you live. This guide doesn't recommend any plan or practice. To compare marketplace plans, start at HealthCare.gov.

## If your employer pays

If your employer pays the DPC fee, that payment isn't counted as taxable income to you. But because it isn't your expense, you can't also reimburse the same fee from your HSA.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

Tax rules like these turn on details. If you're unsure how they apply to you, check with a tax professional.
