## What happened

From 2021 through 2025, extra federal subsidies capped what most marketplace shoppers paid and erased the income limit. Those enhanced credits expired on December 31, 2025. Congress did not extend them.

People felt it right away. In 2026 the average marketplace enrollee's monthly payment rose 58%, from $113 to $178. The average deductible jumped 37%, from $2,759 to $3,786. As people traded down, bronze plans went from 30% of enrollees to 40%.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Insurers' proposed rates for 2027 show another median increase of about 15%.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Step 1: check whether you still qualify

Subsidies did not disappear. The regular premium tax credit still covers household incomes between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty line. What came back is the "cliff." Earn one dollar over 400% and the credit drops to zero.

Coverage for 2027 uses the poverty guidelines HHS published in January 2026:<sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

<div style="overflow-x: auto; margin: 0 0 24px">

| Household size | 100% of poverty line | 400% (the cliff) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,960 | $63,840 |
| 2 | $21,640 | $86,560 |
| 3 | $27,320 | $109,280 |
| 4 | $33,000 | $132,000 |

</div>

Alaska and Hawaii have higher figures.<sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup> The income that counts is your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) for 2027, which you estimate when you apply.

If your income is below 250% of the poverty line, look hard at **silver plans**. Only silver plans carry cost-sharing reductions, which can shrink the deductible to a few hundred dollars. In that income range, silver often beats everything below.

## Step 2: if you are near the cliff, lower the income that counts

Few people know about this move, and it can be worth the most. Money you put into these accounts comes off your MAGI:

- **HSA contributions:** up to $4,500 for one person or $9,000 for a family in 2027.<sup><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup> If you are 55 or older, you can add $1,000, and each spouse puts that extra into their own HSA.<sup><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup> You need an HSA-compatible plan. Since 2026, every individual-market bronze and catastrophic plan counts.<sup><a href="#src-17">17</a></sup>
- **Traditional (pre-tax) 401(k), IRA, SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) contributions.** A traditional IRA deduction can be limited if you or your spouse has a retirement plan at work and your income is above certain levels.<sup><a href="#src-16">16</a></sup>

A hypothetical: a married couple, both 58, expects $90,000 of income in 2027. That puts them about $3,400 over the cliff for a household of two, so they get no subsidy. Now say they put $11,000 into HSAs (the $9,000 family limit plus a $1,000 catch-up for each spouse, each in their own account) and $4,000 into a deductible traditional IRA. Their MAGI falls to about $75,000. They are back under the line. At their age, premiums run high, so the tax credit is often worth thousands of dollars a year.

The exact amount depends on your local benchmark plan. Run your own numbers on HealthCare.gov, and check with a tax professional before you count on this.

## Step 3: compare the realistic options

Each option below gets the same four questions. The first three are regulated ACA insurance.

<div style="overflow-x: auto; margin: 0 0 24px">

| Option | Covers pre-existing conditions | Has an out-of-pocket cap | Works with an HSA | Pairs with DPC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze plan | Yes | Yes, up to $12,000 per person in 2027 | Yes | Strong fit |
| Catastrophic plan | Yes | Yes, deductible equals the cap | Yes | Strong fit |
| Silver plan | Yes | Yes | Only some | Sometimes redundant |
| Short-term plan | Usually not | Often not | No | Marketed with DPC, risky |
| Health care sharing ministry | Often limited | No | No | Marketed with DPC, risky |
| Fixed indemnity | Varies | Not applicable (pays set amounts) | No | Not a substitute |

</div>

### Bronze plan

**What it is:** the lowest-premium ACA metal tier. High deductible, full protections. Preventive care is free before the deductible.

**Who it fits:** people who mainly want protection from a huge bill and can handle small bills themselves, especially with DPC and an HSA.

**Who should skip it:** people under 250% of the poverty line, where a silver plan with cost-sharing reductions may cost about the same and cover far more.

**Costs and risks:** the 2027 out-of-pocket maximum is $12,000 for one person and $24,000 for a family.<sup><a href="#src-13">13</a></sup> A bad year can hit that number.

### Catastrophic plan

**What it is:** a plan whose deductible equals the out-of-pocket cap. It covers preventive care and at least three primary care visits a year before the deductible.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

**Who it fits:** people under 30, and people 30 and older who get a hardship or affordability exemption.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup> For 2026, CMS widened the hardship exemption to people who can't get subsidies because of their income.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup> The 2027 rules are less settled. In July 2026 a federal judge in Maryland paused a 2027 rule that would have opened catastrophic plans to more people. The order did not reach the hardship exemption itself or a new option for catastrophic plans with terms of up to 10 years.<sup><a href="#src-15">15</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> Check your eligibility on HealthCare.gov when you apply.

**Who should skip it:** anyone who qualifies for a premium tax credit. The credit can't be used on a catastrophic plan, so a subsidized bronze or silver plan may cost less.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

**Costs and risks:** you pay full price for most care until you hit the cap.

### Short-term plan

**What it is:** medical insurance that does not follow ACA rules. It can ask health questions, exclude pre-existing conditions, cap benefits and skip an out-of-pocket maximum. In 2025 federal agencies said they would not prioritize enforcing the 2024 limits on these plans, so state law now does most of the policing.<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup>

**Who it fits:** a healthy person with a short, known gap, such as a few months before a new job's coverage starts.

**Who should skip it:** anyone with a health condition, anyone pregnant or planning to be, and anyone who would lean on it for a full year.

**Costs and risks:** premiums are often lower than unsubsidized ACA plans. A claim tied to a condition you had before can be denied, and there may be no limit on what you owe.

### Health care sharing ministry

**What it is:** members share each other's medical bills under faith-based guidelines. Regulators note that ministries "are not insurance and can't guarantee the payment of claims." State insurance regulators don't supervise them, and they don't have to follow ACA protections such as covering pre-existing conditions or capping out-of-pocket costs.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup>

**Who it fits:** members who share the group's beliefs and could pay a large bill on their own if it isn't shared.

**Who should skip it:** anyone who could not absorb a large bill the ministry declines to share.

**Costs and risks:** monthly shares are often lower than unsubsidized premiums. Nothing guarantees a bill gets paid.

### Fixed indemnity

**What it is:** a policy that pays a set dollar amount per event, such as $500 per hospital day, no matter what the bill is. See [Value-added benefits, explained](/blog/value-added-benefits-explained/).

**Who it fits:** people who already have major medical coverage and want extra cash when something happens.

**Who should skip it:** anyone thinking of it as their only coverage. It is a supplement, not health insurance.

**Costs and risks:** premiums are low, but a payout can cover a small slice of a large bill.

## Other doors to check

- **Medicaid:** if your 2027 income will drop, check eligibility. Rules vary by state.
- **A spouse's or employer's plan:** a job change or marriage opens a special enrollment period.
- **ICHRA:** if you own a small business, an individual coverage HRA lets the company reimburse individual plans tax-free.

## Where direct primary care fits

Every path above that ends in bronze or catastrophic leaves you paying for everyday care until you meet the deductible. That is the gap DPC fills, for a flat monthly fee. A 2025 law lets people in certain DPC arrangements keep their HSA and pay the fee from it.<sup><a href="#src-17">17</a></sup> See [DPC plus a bronze plan, with worked numbers](/blog/dpc-plus-bronze-worked-numbers/).

## Key dates and free help

Open enrollment for 2027 on HealthCare.gov runs from **November 1, 2026** to **January 15, 2027**, CMS confirmed after the court ruling.<sup><a href="#src-14">14</a></sup> Choose a plan by **December 15** for coverage that starts January 1. States that run their own exchanges set their own deadlines, so check yours.

You don't have to sort this out alone. HealthCare.gov, your state exchange and local navigators help for free and can show your real prices. For the income moves in Step 2, a tax professional is worth the call.
