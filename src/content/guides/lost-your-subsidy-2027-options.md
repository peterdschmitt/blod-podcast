## What happened

From 2021 through 2025, extra federal subsidies capped what most marketplace shoppers paid and removed the income limit entirely. Those enhanced credits expired on December 31, 2025, and Congress did not extend them.

The result showed up fast. In 2026 the average marketplace enrollee's monthly payment rose 58%, from $113 to $178. The average deductible rose 37%, from $2,759 to $3,786. Bronze plans went from 30% to 40% of enrollees as people traded down.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Insurers' proposed 2027 rates show another median increase of about 15%.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Step 1: check whether you still qualify

Subsidies did not disappear. The regular premium tax credit is still there for household incomes between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty line. What came back is the "cliff": one dollar over 400% and the credit drops to zero.

For 2027 coverage, the line is:<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

<div style="overflow-x: auto; margin: 0 0 24px">

| Household size | 100% of poverty line | 400% (the cliff) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,960 | $63,840 |
| 2 | $21,640 | $86,560 |
| 3 | $27,320 | $109,280 |
| 4 | $33,000 | $132,000 |

</div>

Alaska and Hawaii have higher figures. The income that counts is your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) for 2027, which you estimate when you apply.

If your income is below 250% of the poverty line, also look at **silver plans**. Only silver plans carry cost-sharing reductions, which can lower the deductible to a few hundred dollars. For many people in that range, silver beats every option below.

## Step 2: if you are near the cliff, lower the income that counts

This is the most valuable and least known move. Money you put into these accounts comes off your MAGI:

- **HSA contributions:** up to $4,500 for one person or $9,000 for a family in 2027, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> You need an HSA-compatible plan, and every marketplace bronze and catastrophic plan qualifies.
- **Traditional (pre-tax) 401(k), IRA, SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) contributions.**

Here is a hypothetical. A married couple, both 58, expects $90,000 in 2027 income. That is about $3,400 over the cliff for a household of two, so no subsidy. If they put $11,000 into HSAs (the $9,000 family limit, plus a $1,000 catch-up for each spouse, each into their own account) and $4,000 into a traditional IRA, their MAGI drops to about $75,000. They are back under the line and qualify for a tax credit, which at their age can be worth many thousands of dollars a year. Exact amounts depend on your local benchmark plan, so run your own numbers on HealthCare.gov, and talk to a tax professional before relying on this.

## Step 3: compare the realistic options

Every option below is described the same way. The first three are regulated ACA insurance.

<div style="overflow-x: auto; margin: 0 0 24px">

| Option | Covers pre-existing conditions | Has an out-of-pocket cap | Works with an HSA | Pairs with DPC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze plan | Yes | Yes, up to $12,000 per person in 2027 | Yes | Strong fit |
| Catastrophic plan | Yes | Yes, deductible equals the cap | Yes | Strong fit |
| Silver plan | Yes | Yes | Only some | Sometimes redundant |
| Short-term plan | Usually not | Often not | No | Marketed with DPC, risky |
| Health care sharing ministry | Often limited | No | No | Marketed with DPC, risky |
| Fixed indemnity | Varies | Not applicable (pays set amounts) | No | Not a substitute |

</div>

### Bronze plan

**What it is:** the lowest-premium ACA metal tier. High deductible, full protections. Preventive care is free before the deductible.

**Who it fits:** people who mostly need protection from a big bill and can handle small bills themselves, especially with DPC and an HSA.

**Who should skip it:** people under 250% of the poverty line, where silver with cost-sharing reductions may cost about the same and cover far more.

**Costs and risks:** the 2027 out-of-pocket maximum is $12,000 for one person and $24,000 for a family.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> A bad year can reach that.

### Catastrophic plan

**What it is:** a plan with a deductible equal to the out-of-pocket cap. It covers preventive care and three primary care visits a year before the deductible.<sup><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup>

**Who can buy it:** people under 30, and people with a hardship exemption. CMS widened that exemption to people who cannot get premium tax credits or cost-sharing reductions because of their income.<sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup><sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> The 2027 rule also lets insurers offer catastrophic plans with terms of up to ten years.

**Costs and risks:** you cannot use a premium tax credit on it, so if you qualify for a subsidy, a subsidized bronze plan may cost less.

### Short-term plan

**What it is:** medical insurance that does not follow ACA rules. It can ask health questions, exclude pre-existing conditions, cap benefits and skip an out-of-pocket maximum. Federal agencies said in 2025 they would not prioritize enforcing the 2024 limits, so state rules now matter most.<sup><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup>

**Who it fits:** a healthy person with a short, known gap, such as a few months before a new job's coverage starts.

**Who should skip it:** anyone with a health condition, anyone pregnant or planning to be, and anyone who would rely on it for a full year.

### Health care sharing ministry

**What it is:** members share each other's medical bills under faith-based guidelines. Regulators note that ministries "are not insurance and can't guarantee the payment of claims," state insurance regulators do not supervise them, and they skip ACA protections such as pre-existing condition coverage and out-of-pocket limits.<sup><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup>

**Who should skip it:** anyone who could not absorb a large bill that the ministry declines to share.

### Fixed indemnity

**What it is:** pays a set dollar amount per event, such as $500 per hospital day, no matter what the bill is. Useful as a supplement. Not coverage. See [Value-added benefits, explained](/blog/value-added-benefits-explained/).

## Other doors to check

- **Medicaid:** if your 2027 income will drop, check eligibility. Rules vary by state.
- **A spouse's or employer's plan:** a job change or marriage opens a special enrollment period.
- **ICHRA:** if you own a small business, an individual coverage HRA lets the company reimburse individual plans tax-free.

## Where direct primary care fits

Every scenario above that ends in bronze or catastrophic leaves you paying everyday care out of pocket until the deductible. That is exactly the gap DPC fills, for a flat fee you can now pay from your HSA. See [DPC plus a bronze plan, with worked numbers](/blog/dpc-plus-bronze-worked-numbers/).

## Key dates

Open enrollment for 2027 opens **November 1, 2026** on HealthCare.gov. Choose a plan by **December 15** for coverage starting January 1. A rule that would have shortened the window was blocked in court and is on appeal, so confirm the final deadline on HealthCare.gov or your state exchange, since some states run longer windows.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>
